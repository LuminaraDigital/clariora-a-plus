The problem

Most A+ practice tells you a percentage. The exam does not use one.

"I have done 300 practice questions and I still do not know if I would pass."

Every mock is 90 questions sampled to the official domain weights and scored from 100 to 900 against 675 or 700. You get the same verdict the test centre would give.

"The question pack just shows the right letter. I end up memorising answers."

Every question carries a full explanation and a line on why each distractor is wrong, so a miss teaches the concept, not the letter.

"My study app wants a subscription and a Wi-Fi signal."

Clariora installs as an offline app on Windows, Linux and the browser. No account, no monthly fee, no phone-home.

"I keep re-reading the whole book because I do not know which bits I am weak on."

The readiness score is computed per sub-objective across both blueprints. Weak objectives go to the top of your daily plan and into spaced repetition.

How it works

Three steps. The first one takes twenty minutes.

  1. Open the app

    In the browser or as a desktop install. There is no account to create and nothing to configure.

  2. Take the 20 question diagnostic

    A mixed Core 1 and Core 2 placement under exam conditions. It gives you a first readiness score and a list of weak objectives.

  3. Follow the daily plan until readiness says go

    Drill the weak objectives, review with spaced repetition, and sit full 90 in 90 mocks. When the scaled score clears the pass mark consistently, book the exam.

What you get

Everything the exam will throw at you, with the answer key you wish the book had.

Five fresh mocks per core

636 Core 1 and 494 Core 2 questions mean five full exams each before anything repeats. Flag for review, strike distractors, pause and resume.

Every miss explained

A full explanation and a distractor analysis on all 1,130 questions. Review is never limited, even on the free tier when limits arrive.

Study tools that target the gap

Objective tracker, a study library built from the blueprints, spaced repetition for ports and commands, and a video index for each objective.

A coach when you ask for one

Ghost Coach explains a question in plain language on request. Optional, off by default, and it uses your own Groq key so we never see your data.

Questions

Things people ask before they open it.

Is Clariora new? Does anyone use it yet?
Yes, it is early. There is no customer quote wall yet because we do not invent testimonials. Take the free diagnostic, read the source if you want proof of how scoring works, and email support@datacentre.academy if you pass and want to be quoted with your name and role.
Is it really free?
Yes. Every feature is unlocked with no limits while Clariora is in its 3.x releases. When the personal licence goes on sale, the free tier keeps a daily allowance: the 20 question diagnostic, 20 practice questions, 20 flashcards and one lab per day, plus unlimited review of explanations. Nothing you have already done is taken away.
Do I need an account?
No. Your progress is stored on your device. There is no login, no email capture and no card on file. Cloud sync through Supabase exists as an option and is switched off by default.
How close is the scoring to the real exam?
Each mock draws 90 questions to the official domain weightings for that core and reports a scaled score from 100 to 900. Core 1 passes at 675 and Core 2 at 700, the same marks CompTIA uses. CompTIA does not publish its exact scaling function, so treat the scaled score as a calibrated estimate rather than a guarantee.
How many questions are there, and will they repeat?
1,130 in total: 636 for Core 1 and 494 for Core 2. That is enough for five full 90 question mocks per core before any question repeats. Domain drills and missed-question drills draw from the same bank.
Does it cover the current 220-1201 and 220-1202 objectives?
Yes. Both blueprints are loaded, every question is tagged with the sub-objective it tests, and the build refuses any question whose domain string or objective code does not exist in the official list.
Does it work offline?
Yes. In the browser it installs as a progressive web app and keeps working without a connection. The Windows and Linux desktop builds are fully offline. Only the optional pieces, video playback from YouTube and the Ghost Coach, need a connection.
Which platforms are supported?
Any modern browser, a Windows installer, a Windows portable executable that needs no admin rights, and Linux as an AppImage or a Debian package. macOS builds from source with one command until a signed DMG is published. There is also a terminal exam runner in Python for people who like that sort of thing.
What is the ledger, and why would an instructor care?
Every session is appended to a local ledger where each entry carries the hash of the one before it and is signed with a key generated on that device. Changing an old entry breaks the chain, so an exported record is evidence that the work happened when it says it did.
What is Ghost Coach and does it cost anything?
Ghost Coach is an optional tutor that explains a question when you ask. It is off by default. To turn it on you paste your own Groq API key, which is stored on your device and sent only to Groq. Clariora never sees your questions or your key.
Are the Professor Messer videos included?
Clariora includes an index of 137 of his free videos mapped to the objective each one teaches, and links to them on YouTube. The videos are not copied or redistributed.
When does the personal licence go on sale, and what changes?
There is no date yet. When it does, the free tier gains the daily limits described above and a 39 USD one-time key removes them on that PC. There is no subscription and no yearly plan. The price will not drop below 29 USD, so there is nothing to gain by waiting.
Can I read the source code?
Yes. Clariora is published under the GNU Affero General Public License v3.0 on GitHub, including the scoring engine, the question validator and the tests that run on every commit.
What happens to my data?
It stays on your device. Usage telemetry and cloud sync both exist in the code and both are off by default. The web edition is served from Cloudflare with a strict content security policy and no third-party scripts.
